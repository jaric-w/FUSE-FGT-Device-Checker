The second life of a laptop.

What happens after you drop one off at 180 Sudbury Street.

$100A quarter of our refurbished computers sell for less than this.

  1. It comes through our door.

    You bring it to the shop on a Friday or Saturday, or a business books a pickup for a whole batch.

  2. Every drive is wiped.

    Data is securely erased with industry-standard tools. Then memory, drives and batteries are tested one by one.

  3. Repaired and set up.

    Volunteers and trainees fix or upgrade parts and install Linux Mint, a free system that runs well on older machines.

  4. $100A quarter of our refurbished computers sell for less than this.

    Back in someone's hands.

    It goes to a student, a newcomer, a senior or a family at a price they can afford.

Not a scrapyard.

Without reuse, working computers end up in heaps like this one in Ottawa. Whatever we can't refurbish is sent for proper recycling.

400kpounds of electronics recycled responsibly instead of thrown away.

Retiring company laptops?

Give 10 or more similar, working computers and pickup is free. You get proof that every drive was wiped, and a report on where they went.
